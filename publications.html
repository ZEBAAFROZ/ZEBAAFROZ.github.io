  1. EACL
    Reasoning for Propaganda Detection via Label-Guided CoT Optimisation
    Zeba Afroz, Harsh Vardhan, and Md. Shad Akhtar
    Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL 2027 (to appear)
    arXiv PDF
    Propaganda detection systems usually operate as black boxes, lacking explanations. To address this, we hypothesise that persuasive techniques contain definitional characteristics with respect to their social-political setting. We introduce ReCoT, a reasoning-conditioned detection framework that captures definitional cues of persuasive techniques via population-level refinement and performance-guided resampling, without requiring additional explanation annotations. On INDI-PROPspan, ReCoT achieves a macro F1LCS of 0.364, surpassing baselines and reaching 88% of the SFT upper bound on Avg F1LCS. Similarly, on PTC, ReCoT reaches 85% of the SFT upper bound on Avg F1LCS, closing much of this gap while remaining interpretable.

  2. LREC
    Fine-grained Narrative Classification in Biased News Articles
    Zeba Afroz, Harsh Vardhan, Pawan Bhakuni, Aanchal Punia, Rajdeep Kumar, and Md. Shad Akhtar
    Proceedings of the Fifteenth Language Resources and Evaluation Conference (LREC 2026), pp. 8431–8445, Palma, Mallorca, Spain. ELRA
    DOI PDF arXiv Paper page
    We introduce INDI-PROP, the first ideologically grounded fine-grained narrative dataset for Indian news media, covering 1,266 articles on two polarizing socio-political events (CAA and the Farmers' protest), annotated at three hierarchical levels. We propose FANTA and TPTC, GPT-4o-mini guided multi-hop reasoning frameworks for article bias, narrative, and persuasive technique classification, yielding substantial improvements over baselines.

  1. arXiv
    Incongruence Identification in Eyewitness Testimony
    Akshara Nair, Zeba Afroz, and Md. Shad Akhtar
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.05650, 2025
    arXiv PDF
    We introduce the task of detecting incongruences between eyewitness testimonies, and MIND (MultI-EyewitNess Deception), a dataset of 2,927 contextually related answer pairs capturing explicit and implicit contradictions. Our instruction-tuned incongruity detection framework, INTEND, grounded in investigative 6W and multi-hop reasoning, achieves a +5.63% F1-score improvement over fine-tuned and prompt-tuning baselines.
