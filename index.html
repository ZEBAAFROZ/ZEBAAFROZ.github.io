Hello! I am Zeba Afroz, a Ph.D. Research Scholar at the FLaMe Research Lab at IIIT-Delhi, India, supervised by Dr. Md. Shad Akhtar. My research lies at the intersection of Natural Language Processing (NLP), computational journalism, and social computing.

My work focuses on understanding how language is used to shape narratives — whether through ideological framing in news media, incongruences in eyewitness accounts, or figurative expression in online content. I am particularly interested in building robust AI systems that can reason about bias, deception, and narrative intent in real-world text.

I completed my M.Tech. in Artificial Intelligence at J.C. Bose University of Science and Technology, YMCA and my B.Tech. at Punjab Technical University.

I am always open to collaborations in NLP and computational social science — feel free to reach out if you'd like to discuss research ideas.

    Fine-grained Narrative Classification in Biased News Articles
    Zeba Afroz, Harsh Vardhan, Pawan Bhakuni, Aanchal Punia, Rajdeep Kumar, and Md. Shad Akhtar
    Proceedings of the Joint International Conference on Computational Linguistics, Language Resources and Evaluation (LREC), 2026
    arXiv PDF
    We propose a fine-grained narrative classification framework for biased news articles, with article-bias classification as a precursor task alongside fine-grained persuasive technique identification. We introduce INDI-PROP, the first ideologically grounded fine-grained narrative dataset with multi-level annotation for propaganda in Indian news, covering 1,266 articles across two polarizing socio-political events (CAA and the Farmers' protest), annotated at three hierarchical levels.

  2. arXiv
    Incongruence Identification in Eyewitness Testimony
    Akshara Nair, Zeba Afroz, and Md. Shad Akhtar
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.05650, 2025
    arXiv PDF
    We introduce the task of detecting incongruences between eyewitness testimonies. Given Q&A exchange pairs from two subjects, the system identifies contextually related inconsistencies and marks their spans. We introduce MIND (MultI-EyewitNess Deception), a dataset of 2,927 contextually related answer pairs, and the INTEND framework, grounded in investigative 6W and multi-hop reasoning.
